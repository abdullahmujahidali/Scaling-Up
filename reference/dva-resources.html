Tier 1 — Official AWS (trust these above everything else)

AWS wrote the exam. So AWS's own wording is worth more than anyone else's. If AWS documentation and some YouTube video disagree, the documentation is right.

Resource What it is, and what's inside How much time
Official Exam Guide The contract — AWS's written promise about what can and cannot appear on the exam. It lists the four domains, their percentages, and the "task statements" (exactly what gets tested). The most valuable part: the "out of scope" list, which tells you what you do not need to study. That saves you time. Once, 30 minutes. Read all of it — it's short. Mark each task statement: ✅ know it / 😐 shaky / ❌ no idea. Those marks are your study plan.
AWS Skill Builder — free official practice questions 20 practice questions written by AWS itself, free. This is the closest thing to real exam wording available for free. Their value isn't the answers — it's seeing how AWS phrases a question: how long, how indirect, how similar the options look. Once, about a week before the exam. Not for scoring — for getting used to the wording. Only 20 questions exist, so don't waste them: do them when you're already partly prepared.
Certification page Registration, price (150 USD), the retake rule (14-day wait after a fail), and the official sample-questions PDF. This isn't a reading resource — it's a doing resource. 10 minutes — today. Open it only to book the exam.
Well-Architected Framework This is the exam's hidden answer key. When a question asks "what is best practice" or "the AWS-recommended approach", it means this document. It's why the answer is always "IAM role" not "access key", and "managed service" not "run it yourself on EC2". Only the Security and Operational Excellence pillars, ~40 minutes. Don't read the whole framework — it's huge and mostly aimed at the SAA exam.

Tier 2 — the actual content (this is where your time should go)

Stephane Maarek's Udemy course — your #1 job
  • You are 88% complete. The remaining 12% is the easiest, highest-value work on this entire list. Finish it before starting any new resource.
  • ⚠️ The last sections are the most heavily tested. Courses typically end with CI/CD, CloudFormation, and security — that's Domains 2 + 3, which are 50% of the exam. Your remaining 12% may cover the heaviest part of the test.
  • Watch at 1.25x–1.5x, but hands-on demos at normal speed. If the English is hard to follow, turn English subtitles ON — reading while listening improves comprehension a lot.
  • Do not re-watch videos as revision. This is the most common mistake. Re-watching gives a false feeling of "I know this". Use these notes and practice exams for revision instead.
Practice exams — these are what pass the exam
  • You already have 15 full-length exams (975 questions) right here — see the exams page. They're free and deliberately harder than the real exam, so 75–80% means you pass the real one comfortably.
  • If you want more, the Stephane Maarek or Neal Davis Udemy practice exams are the best purchase available. But finish the 15 here first — those are free.
  • ⭐ The single most important rule: an exam takes 1 hour, but you should spend 2 hours reviewing the wrong answers. Most people take an exam, look at the score, and start the next one — that teaches nothing. The learning is in the review, not the exam.
Braindumps — stay away Some websites claim to have "real exam questions" (ExamTopics, dumps, and similar). Avoid them for two reasons: (1) the answers are frequently wrong — you would memorise incorrect material. (2) The AWS Certification Agreement prohibits them; if caught, your certification can be revoked and you can be banned. The 975 questions here are written from the exam guide's task statements — not copied from the real exam. That distinction protects you.

Tier 3 — open only when you're stuck

This is documentation. Documentation is not meant to be read cover to cover — it's a dictionary. Search it when a practice question confuses you. Never plan "today I'll read the Lambda docs".

When to open it Link
A Lambda limit, trigger, or error behaviour confuses you. Domain 1 is 32% of the exam and Lambda is its centre. Lambda Developer Guide
Partition keys, LSI vs GSI, capacity modes, or streams confuse you. Heavily tested. DynamoDB Developer Guide
⭐ You get a policy-evaluation question wrong. These are guaranteed exam points — the rule is simple: explicit deny > explicit allow > implicit deny. IAM Policy Evaluation Logic
You can't recall a number. But check our own Numbers sheet first — it's the compressed version with only exam-relevant figures. AWS Service Quotas
Documentation is ambiguous and you want to know what actually happens. AWS's own Q&A forum. AWS re:Post

People — worth more than the links

Abdullah Ajmal (your team lead) The most valuable resource on this entire list, and the one still unused. He holds this exact certification (March 2025) and knows your context — which no website does. Ask him three things: (1) Does the company reimburse the 150 USD, or is there a company voucher? (2) Which practice exams did he use? (3) How did the real exam compare to the practice ones? The first question might save you 150 dollars — so ask it first.

r/AWSCertifications — people post detailed "passed DVA-C02, here's what was actually on it" write-ups almost daily. Search it before booking. Upside: you learn what's showing up this month. Downside: some posts are scary — don't spend much time there.
