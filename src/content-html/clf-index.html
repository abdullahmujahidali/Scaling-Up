📖 Lessons

C1 · Shared Responsibility Model

The most guaranteed question on this exam: what AWS is responsible for and what you are. One rule that works on every version of the question — plus where the line moves with the service (EC2 vs RDS).

C2 · Support plans, billing & cost tools

⭐ Your highest-value lesson. Support plans, Cost Explorer vs Budgets, Organizations, cost allocation tags. The part writing code never exposes you to — so it's the most direct move from guessing to knowing.

C3 · Pricing models

On-Demand, Reserved, Spot, Savings Plans, Dedicated Hosts. One question decides them all: can this work be interrupted? Plus data transfer costs and S3 storage tiers.

C7 · Everything else (gap-filler)

I compared the lessons against AWS's full in-scope list; this covers what C1–C6 left out — AMIs, EBS snapshots, S3 versioning, Elastic IPs, Savings Plans, Service Catalog, Local Zones and the rest. (Aug 2026 audit: 18 services were still missing after this — see C8/C9.)

C8 · Four things the earlier lessons left out

The audit against your slides found these four completely missing — and all four are common on the exam: the load balancer types (ALB/NLB/GWLB), the three parts of CloudWatch (metrics/alarms/logs), SQS decoupling and FIFO, and Route 53 routing policies.

C4 · Cloud Concepts & the Well-Architected Framework

Domain 1 (24%). The benefits of cloud, the 6 Well-Architected pillars, CapEx vs OpEx, elasticity vs scalability. The easiest 24% on the exam — it asks for vocabulary, not technical depth.

C5 · IAM & the security services

The other half of Domain 2 (30%). Root user protection, IAM roles vs access keys, MFA, least privilege — plus the security services: WAF, Shield, GuardDuty, Inspector, Macie, KMS, Artifact.

C6 · The core AWS services

Domain 3 (34%) — the exam's largest. Regions/AZs/Edge, compute, storage, databases, networking and the rest. Your Django stack mapped onto AWS names, which makes this the most natural lesson for you.
