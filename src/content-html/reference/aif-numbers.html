AIF Terms & Trigger Words

AIF barely asks numbers — it asks words and service names. So this sheet gives you three things instead: trigger words (word → answer), a one-line job per service, and the pairs that blur together.

Print this one ⭐ = most frequently tested
How to use this sheet Do the ⭐ rows first. Look daily, but test yourself first, then look.

Specifically for you: the first table pays the most. Most AIF questions turn on a single word — recognise it and the answer arrives without fully parsing the sentence.

The pairs that blur together

PairThe differenceThe phrase to hold
⭐ RAG vs Fine-tuningRAG = new information. Fine-tuning = new manner."What it knows" vs "how it speaks".
⭐ Guardrails vs ClarifyGuardrails = safety, forward-looking (GenAI). Clarify = fairness & explainability, backward-looking (ML).Guardrails stops it speaking; Clarify explains why it spoke.
⭐ Bedrock vs SageMakerBedrock = use someone else's model. SageMaker = build your own.Hire a chef vs build a kitchen.
Training vs InferenceTraining = learning (expensive, once). Inference = using (cheap, per request).migrate vs runserver.
Overfitting vs UnderfittingOverfitting = memorised (good on training, bad on new). Underfitting = barely learned (bad everywhere).Rote memorising vs not studying at all.
Transcribe vs PollyTranscribe = speech → text. Polly = text → speech.Polly = parrot = speaks.
Transparency vs ExplainabilityTransparency = disclosure about the model (Model Cards). Explainability = why a decision happened (Clarify)."What is it" vs "why did it do that".

The night before the exam Look only at the Trigger Words and pairs tables. Don't read anything new. Remember: 700/1000 overall, no per-domain pass needed, and no penalty for a wrong answer — so never leave a blank.
