Which AWS AI service? — matching name to job

A large part of this exam simply asks: "which service for this job?" The good news is that each service has one job, and the name often tells you. This lesson builds that pairing: job → service.

~12 min One concept: one service, one job Win: the most common question type on the exam
First, recall from I1: when do you use RAG and when fine-tuning? And what's the difference between training and inference?
RAG = new information (fresh data, your own documents, reducing hallucinations). Fine-tuning = new manner (style, tone, specialist domain). Training = learning (expensive, once); inference = using (cheap, every request). Today we attach AWS names to these jobs.

If you remember one thing Learn two big names; everything else is a small single-job service:

⭐ Amazon Bedrock = for GenAI. Using foundation models (Claude, Titan, etc.) via an API. No servers, no training. GenAI / LLM / chatbot / RAG in a question = Bedrock.

⭐ Amazon SageMaker AI = for building, training and deploying your own ML model. "Train a custom model", "data scientists", "full control" = SageMaker.

The simple rule: Bedrock = use someone else's model. SageMaker = build your own.

The story: three ways to feed people

Imagine feeding 100 people. First way: buy ready-made packets — open and serve. No effort, but you get exactly what's in the packet. These are AWS's managed AI services — Rekognition, Transcribe, Comprehend. One job each, instant, no expertise needed.

Second way: hire an experienced chef who already knows how to cook everything — you just tell them what to make and how. This is Bedrock. The model is already trained; you supply the prompt.

Third way: build your own kitchen, hire your own chef, write your own recipes. The most work and cost, but total control. This is SageMaker. The exam checks you can pick the right one — and in most questions the answer is the least effort option that meets the need.

⭐ The managed AI services — one service, one job
  • Amazon Rekognition = images and video (faces, objects, unsafe content). The name says it: recognition. Image/video in a question = this.
  • Amazon Transcribe = speech to text. A written record of a meeting recording.
  • Amazon Polly = text to speech. The reverse of Transcribe. (Polly = a parrot, which speaks — use that to remember.)
  • Amazon Translate = one language to another.
  • ⭐ Amazon Comprehend = understanding text — sentiment (positive/negative), entities (names, places), language detection, and finding PII (personal data). "Sentiment of customer reviews" = Comprehend.
  • Amazon Lex = building chatbots (the technology behind Alexa). Voice and text.
  • Amazon Textract = extracting text from scanned documents and forms ("text" + "extract"). Invoices, forms, PDFs.
  • Amazon Personalize = recommendations ("customers also bought"). Amazon Forecast = predicting future values (sales, demand). Amazon Fraud Detector = detecting fraud. All three are named after their job.
  • Amazon Q = AWS's own AI assistant (for business questions and for developers). Kiro and Amazon Quick also appear as newer names in the exam guide.
⭐ The names inside Bedrock — these are distinct things
  • ⭐ Bedrock Knowledge Bases = AWS's name for RAG. If a question is about building RAG, this is the answer. It handles chunking, embedding and search for you.
  • ⭐ Bedrock Guardrails = safety limits — blocking harmful content, denying topics, masking PII. "Prevent harmful content" or "safety" in a question = Guardrails, always.
  • Bedrock Agents = letting the model take actions (calling tools, multi-step work). Bedrock AgentCore is the newer name in the exam guide.
  • Bedrock Model Evaluation = testing and comparing models. Bedrock Prompt Management = storing and versioning prompts.
⭐ The names inside SageMaker — the Responsible AI answers
  • ⭐ SageMaker Clarify = detecting bias and providing explainability (why the model decided that). The most-asked name in Domain 4. "Detect bias" = Clarify.
  • ⭐ SageMaker Model Monitor = watching the model in production — catching drift as its quality degrades over time.
  • ⭐ Amazon Augmented AI (A2I) = putting a human in the loop for review. "Human-in-the-loop" or "human review" = A2I.
  • SageMaker Model Cards = documentation of a model (what it is, what data it used, its intended use). The transparency answer.
  • SageMaker JumpStart = pre-built models and solutions to start quickly. SageMaker Ground Truth = getting your data labelled.
Exam English — the language of service-picking questions These questions come in a standard shape. Learn these words:

"with the LEAST development effort" / "LEAST operational overhead" = pick the lowest-effort answer — a ready-made managed service, not a custom SageMaker model.
"without managing infrastructure" = no servers = Bedrock or a managed service.
"pre-trained" = already trained (you don't train it).
"custom model" = your own model = SageMaker.
"out of the box" = works as-is, with nothing built.
"in the loop" = included in the process (human-in-the-loop = a person also checks).
"drift" = the model degrading or shifting over time.

Bridge to your work You make this call weekly, only the names differ. When you need payments in Django you reach for Stripe's SDK — you don't write a payment system. When you need email you take SES or SendGrid.

The same reasoning applies here: a managed service = importing a library (Comprehend, Rekognition — one line, done). Bedrock = an API call that rents intelligence. SageMaker = writing it yourself — full control, full responsibility.

And the exam leans the way a good engineer leans: unless there's a specific reason, take the ready-made thing. Which is why, whenever "LEAST effort" appears, the SageMaker option is almost always wrong.

Your win today This is the exam's most common question shape — "which service for this job?" — and you now have one sentence for every major name. It pays across Domains 1, 3 and 4.
