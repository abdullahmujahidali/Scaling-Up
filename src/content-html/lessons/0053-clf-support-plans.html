Support plans: the domain your day job never teaches you

Domain 4 is only 12% — but it's your highest-value lesson. The reason is simple: you have never bought an AWS support plan. This isn't learned by writing code; it's learned by memorising it once.

~10 min One concept: which plan, and why Win: ~6 questions you'd otherwise guess
First, recall from C1: is AWS responsible for security OF the cloud or IN the cloud? And who patches the guest OS on EC2?
AWS = OF the cloud (what you cannot touch). You = IN the cloud. EC2's guest OS is yours — RDS's is AWS's. Today we move to the thing you buy with money: support.

⚠️ Read this first — or you'll memorise the wrong thing In December 2025 AWS renamed and restructured its support plans. The AWS website now shows Business Support+ and Unified Operations, and Developer, Business and Enterprise On-Ramp are being discontinued on 1 January 2027.

But the CLF-C02 exam guide still asks for the OLD names — Developer, Business, Enterprise On-Ramp, Enterprise. The exam guide's own words are: "AWS Developer Support, AWS Business Support, AWS Enterprise On-Ramp Support, AWS Enterprise Support".

So: memorise the OLD ladder below for the exam. If you go and read aws.amazon.com/premiumsupport today, you'll find new names that won't appear on your exam — and you'll get confused. This trap is real. Learn the new names for your job, not for the exam.
If you remember one thing Each plan buys one thing. Remember that one thing, not the whole list:

Basic = free, no technical support (billing only).
Developer = one person, business hours, test/dev only.
Business = ⭐ 24×7 phone and chat + the full set of Trusted Advisor checks. The first plan fit for production.
Enterprise On-Ramp = a pool of TAMs (not one named person).
Enterprise = ⭐ your own designated TAM + 15-minute response.

The most common exam question: "production workload, needs 24×7 support, cheapest plan?" → Business. And "needs a designated TAM" → Enterprise. Those two pairs solve most of them.

The story: hospital cover

Imagine five kinds of health cover. Basic (free): no doctor at all, you may only ask reception what the bill is. Developer: there is a doctor, but only during office hours, and only for minor issues — nobody at night. Business: the phone is answered 24 hours and any family member may call — the first cover you can actually rely on.

Enterprise On-Ramp: a team of doctors is available to you, but you may get a different one each time. Enterprise: you have your own personal doctor who knows your name and your whole history, and in an emergency arrives within 15 minutes. Expensive — but if your life depends on it, that's what you need.

The exam asks in exactly this shape: it describes a situation ("mission-critical production workload", or "a test environment only") and asks which cover fits. Once you can read the situation, the question is over.

The rest of Domain 4's tested facts
  • ⭐ Trusted Advisor: Basic and Developer get core checks only. Business, Enterprise On-Ramp and Enterprise get the full checks. Its five categories: cost optimisation, performance, security, fault tolerance, service limits (quotas).
  • ⭐ Cost Explorer vs Budgets — this pair gets asked. Cost Explorer = looking BACK (see and analyse what you already spent; graphs; 12-month forecast). Budgets = looking FORWARD (set a limit and get alerted before you cross it). "Alert" or "notify when spending exceeds" = Budgets. "Analyse" or "visualise past costs" = Cost Explorer.
  • AWS Pricing Calculator = estimating cost before you build something. (The old "Simple Monthly Calculator" is gone.)
  • ⭐ AWS Organizations consolidated billing: one bill for all accounts — and usage is aggregated for volume discounts, so the total costs less. Reserved Instance and Savings Plans benefits are also shared across the organization. That's the "why consolidate" answer.
  • Cost allocation tags = labels on resources (e.g. Project=alpha) so the bill shows which team or project spent what. Two kinds: AWS-generated (prefixed aws:) and user-defined (yours). They must be activated before they appear in billing reports.
  • AWS Health Dashboard = whether AWS's own services are broken and how that affects your account. AWS Trust & Safety team = where you report abuse of AWS resources. Both become small questions.
  • Where help comes from: re:Post (community Q&A, free), Knowledge Center (fixes for common issues), Prescriptive Guidance (AWS's recommended approaches), Professional Services and Solutions Architects (paid/assigned help), AWS Marketplace (buying third-party software).
Exam English — words that recur in Domain 4 "designated" = assigned specifically to you (your own person). Seeing this word, think Enterprise — it's the biggest single giveaway.
"mission-critical" / "business-critical" = the business stops without it. So: the highest plan.
"impaired" = not completely down, but not working properly. ("Down" = fully stopped — and gets a faster response time.)
"consolidated billing" = one bill for many accounts.
"forecast" = a prediction of what's ahead.
"incur" = to accumulate a cost ("charges are incurred" = it costs money).
"waive" = to cancel or forgive (a fee).
"entitlement" = a licence or right to use something (appears in Marketplace questions).

Bridge to your work This domain feels new, but you've seen the decision before. When your company bought a paid Sentry tier or a managed database plan, the question was identical: is the free tier enough, or do we need paid because someone must be wakeable at night?

Support plans are no different. Developer plan = staging. Business plan = production. Enterprise = the system where the CEO gets called when it's down. The exam only checks whether you can read the scenario and tell staging from production.

And Budgets vs Cost Explorer? That's exactly the difference between alerting and a dashboard — PagerDuty (warns you ahead) versus Grafana (shows you what happened).
From memory: cheapest plan with 24×7 support? Which plan has a "designated TAM"? Enterprise's response time? Does Budgets look forward or back? And which plan first gives the full Trusted Advisor checks?
Cheapest 24×7 = Business. Designated TAM = Enterprise. Enterprise = 15 minutes. Budgets = forward (Cost Explorer = backward). Full Trusted Advisor checks start at Business. All five? Then most of Domain 4 is now yours — and this was the domain you would have guessed on.
